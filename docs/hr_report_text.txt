Abstract
The present work is part of a graduation project carried out within the company Tunis Call Center in order to obtain the National Engineering Degree at the Nabeul Private Higher School of Information Technology and Management (ITBS). The objective of this project is to design and develop an intelligent Human Resources management platform dedicated to client management, operations management, recruitment, and employee followup. The developed solution centralizes several HR processes and provides functionalities such as candidate management, interview management, reporting, and analytics. The platform also integrates Artificial Intelligence techniques for resume analysis and candidate ranking in order to improve recruitment efficiency and support decision-making. The application was designed using the MVC architectural pattern and developed following the Scrum agile methodology. The final result is a modern and scalable web platform that contributes to the digitalization and automation of HR processes. Keywords-- Human Resources Management, Recruitment Platform, Artificial Intelligence, Profil Scraping, Candidate Ranking, MVC Architecture, Scrum

Introduction
From paper to digital transformation is the objective of many companies seeking to save time, optimize processes, facilitate daily work, and reduce dependency on paper-based operations. As businesses scale, the complexity of HR management increases significantly, turning processes that once worked for small teams into operational bottlenecks in larger organizations.
In addition, using Deep Learning in recruitment pipelines is reshaping the way companies identify and hire talent. Over the past years, numerous studies and vendors have explored AI-driven solutions for sourcing, screening, and hiring automation, confirming that this evolution is not just a trend but a structural change in recruitment practices.
In this context, the Digital Pole of Tunis Call Center initiated this project to develop a centralized human resources management platform designed to support daily operations while improving productivity and communication between departments. The objective is not only to digitize traditional processes but also to build a flexible system that simplifies the management of employees, clients, operations, and recruitment activities.
This project is structured around three main modules: the Operations module, the Client module, and the Recruitment module. Each module addresses specific business needs while remaining integrated within a unified workflow.
The development of this system is organized into two main releases. Release 1 focuses on the implementation of the Operations and Client modules, covering employee assignments, client management, scheduling concepts, and operational tracking. Release 2 is dedicated to the Recruitment module, including recruitment workflow automation, candidate management, and AI-assisted candidate scoring and analysis.
The remainder of this report is structured as follows: the first release presents the design, implementation, and testing of the Operations and Client modules, while the second release details the Recruitment module along with the integration of artificial intelligence techniques. Each release includes backlog definition, use case and sequence diagrams, system design, database implementation, and application interfaces.
Through this project, the company aims to accelerate its digital transformation by improving internal organization, reducing repetitive manual tasks, and creating a more efficient working environment adapted to modern business needs.

1.1 Introduction
The project study, often referred to as the preliminary phase, marks a decisive moment in shaping the architecture of the application to be developed. It serves as a compass, guiding our understanding of the requirements while revealing opportunities for refinement and innovation.
To begin this journey, we will set the general context in which the project takes form.
We will then articulate the problem at hand, before unveiling the solution envisioned to address it.
1.2 General Project Context
In an era where organizations strive to modernize their operations, enhancing and digitizing the recruitment process has become a strategic priority. Streamlining talent acquisition workflows, improving efficiency, and ensuring better decision-making are at the heart of this transformation. As part of our final-year project at ITBS, undertaken to obtain an Engineering degree in Software Engineering, we are required to carry out a multimedia project within a professional environment through an internship. This experience is designed to immerse us in real-world professional practices, allowing us to gain practical experience and to apply the theoretical knowledge acquired throughout our academic journey. Within this context, we were tasked with developing a web-based Human Resources Management application. While the system includes several complementary modules such as employee requests, attendance tracking, and planning, the primary focus of this project lies in the recruitment module. This core component aims to enhance and digitize the recruitment pipeline, from candidate application to selection, thereby optimizing the overall hiring process. To successfully achieve these objectives, it is essential to conduct a preliminary study. This study allows us, first, to analyze existing systems and practices, and second, to clearly define the scope and key aspects on which our solution will concentrate. We therefore begin by presenting the host organization.

2.9 Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)
Machine learning models, especially complex models such as ensemble methods or deep learning, often behave as black-box systems. Although these models can achieve high predictive performance, understanding how they reach their decisions remains difficult. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) aims to address this issue by providing techniques that help interpret model predictions and improve transparency and trust in AI systems [Ribeiro et al., 2016, Lundberg and Lee, 2017]. In the context of recruitment and candidate ranking systems, explainability is particularly important. Automated decision systems may influence hiring processes, therefore it is necessary to understand which candidate features (e.g., skills, experience, education) contribute the most to the model's predictions. XAI techniques allow researchers and recruiters to interpret these decisions and verify that the model does not rely on biased or irrelevant features. Among the most widely used XAI techniques are SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations). These methods are considered popular tools for explaining machine learning models, especially when working with tabular data [Ribeiro et al., 2016, Lundberg and Lee, 2017].
2.9.1 SHAP
SHAP is an explainability method based on concepts from game theory. It interprets each feature as a "player" contributing to the final prediction of the model. The method assigns a contribution score (SHAP value) to each feature, indicating how much that feature influences the prediction outcome. One of the main advantages of SHAP is that it can provide both global explanations (importance of features across the entire dataset) and local explanations (importance for a specific prediction) [Lundberg and Lee, 2017]. In recruitment systems, SHAP can be used to identify which candidate attributes (such as technical skills, years of experience, or education level) have the strongest influence on the ranking or recommendation generated by the model.
2.9.2 LIME
LIME is another widely used XAI method designed to explain predictions locally. Instead of analyzing the entire model, LIME focuses on a single prediction and approximates the complex model with a simpler interpretable model around that specific instance [Ribeiro et al., 2016]. For example, in a candidate matching system, LIME can explain why a particular candidate was recommended for a specific job by highlighting the features that contributed positively or negatively to that prediction.

2.10.4 Justification of the Selected Solution
At the initial stage of this project, Selenium was selected as the primary tool for data collection due to its ability to interact with dynamic web applications and simulate real user behavior. This approach proved effective in extracting data from LinkedIn profiles, as Selenium can render JavaScript, navigate pages, and automate actions such as scrolling and clicking. However, during implementation, several limitations emerged. LinkedIn progressively strengthened its anti-bot mechanisms, resulting in frequent request blocking, CAPTCHA challenges, and temporary IP restrictions. These protections are part of LinkedIn's effort to prevent automated data extraction and protect user data. From a legal and ethical perspective, scraping LinkedIn using browser automation tools such as Selenium may violate the platform's Terms of Service, which explicitly restrict automated access, data extraction, and the use of bots without prior authorization. Although scraping publicly accessible data is not always strictly illegal, it may expose the system to compliance risks, including account suspension, IP banning, or potential legal disputes depending on the scale and usage of the data. Due to these technical and compliance challenges, relying solely on Selenium was no longer a reliable or sustainable solution. As a result, alternative approaches were explored. A prepaid API solution, SerpAPI, was identified as a more robust alternative for retrieving LinkedIn profile URLs indirectly through search engine queries. Instead of directly scraping LinkedIn, SerpAPI provides structured results from search engines such as Google, significantly reducing the risk of blocking and simplifying data acquisition. Consequently, a hybrid approach was adopted. SerpAPI is used to efficiently collect LinkedIn profile links, while Selenium is utilized only when necessary to extract additional publicly available information. This combination reduces the scraping footprint on LinkedIn, improves system reliability, and ensures better alignment with platform usage policies. Therefore, while Selenium remains a powerful tool for dynamic data extraction, integrating SerpAPI provides a more stable, scalable, and compliant solution for implementing the recruitment data collection module.

3.4.3 MVC System Architecture
The MVC architecture is a design pattern widely used in software development to organize and structure applications. The main objective of MVC is to separate the application logic into three distinct components, each located in separate files. Model: This component manages the business logic. It handles data management, including storage and the rules governing these data. Its objective is to provide a simple interface for the controller to interact with the data. View: This component is responsible for the presentation layer. It retrieves data and variables in order to display the appropriate information to the user. Controller: This component manages interactions with the user. It acts as an intermediary between the user, the model, and the view. The controller receives user requests, processes them by calling the appropriate methods in the model, and then sends the results to the view for display. 3.4.4 Hardware Architecture
The three-tier architecture is composed of three main layers. In this context, and according to the philosophy behind this architecture, it is more appropriate to refer to functional layers, where each layer is associated with a logical component or entity. [Memoire Online, 2009] Thus, in the three-tier model, we distinguish the following layers:
28

1. Presentation Layer (User Interface): This layer is associated with the client, which is considered a "thin client" since it does not perform any processing tasks, unlike the traditional client-server (two-tier) model.
2. Application Layer (Business Logic): This layer is associated with the server and includes the application server (middleware). In many cases, it is a web server enhanced with application-specific extensions.
3. Data Layer: This layer is associated with the database server (Database Management System (DBMS)), which is responsible for storing and managing data.

5.6.5 Explainability using SHAP and LIME

5.6.5.2 SHAP Global Interpretation

· Positive factors increasing the ranking score · Negative factors decreasing the ranking score · Feature contribution importance This local interpretability improves recruiter trust and supports transparent AI-assisted recruitment decisions. 5.6.5.4 Explainability Synthesis The integration of SHAP and LIME transforms the recruitment platform into an explainable AI system.
